Your mouse and keyboard

Agents work on their window in the background first, so your cursor stays put and the app you are using keeps the keyboard. Some apps only respond to a real mouse or keyboard. Choose when an agent may use yours for those.

Finding controls

Some apps draw their own buttons, so Windows can't tell an agent what they are. Reading the text on screen lets an agent find a button by its label. The text is read on this PC with the OCR built into Windows, only in the window the agent has been given, and nothing is sent anywhere.

An icon without text can be found by asking a vision model where it is, for example "the gear icon". The model sees the agent's window.

App profiles

Overshoulder keeps a profile of each app agents use: its windows and controls, which ways of pressing and typing work in it, its shortcuts, and notes agents leave. The next agent starts from it and works faster. A profile never holds window titles, what was typed or what a field showed, and it never lets an agent do more than you allow.

Tutorials

Tutorials, GIFs, step pictures and annotated screenshots carry a small mark at the end of their caption strip. Their files say they were made with Overshoulder in their metadata either way.

Automatic detection

Lets an agent that is waiting on you notice when you have finished, without you typing anything. Detection only reads window titles, on-screen controls and files. It never clicks or types.

What agents may watch for

Untick anything you don't want agents to watch.

Approvals on your phone

When an agent asks to do something consequential, a box on this PC asks you. You can be asked on your phone at the same time: the first answer counts, and no answer in time is No. Your phone is told what the agent wants to do, the program's name and when the question expires, and nothing else unless you tick the boxes at the bottom. This PC only sends requests out; nothing on it listens for the phone.

Safety

  • Panic key: Ctrl+Alt+P stops the driving agent; resume with overshoulder resume.
  • While you use your mouse or keyboard, agents keep to background work, and anything that needs your input waits until you have been idle. Touching them during such an action stops it.
